The panel

Everything the app can tell you, without opening it.

Most launchers answer a long press with a few shortcuts. Arcadia adds the app's notifications and one of its widgets, and keeps the panel open until you lift your finger.

  1. 01

    The notifications it sent you

    Live ones and the ones you already swiped away, kept per app and scrollable inside the panel.

  2. 02

    The shortcuts it publishes

    Whatever the app offers: a scene, a chat, a search. No loading screen first.

  3. 03

    One of its widgets, running

    Pick a widget per app and it runs inside the panel, updating as it would on the home screen.

Two screens

Open the phone and nothing jumps.

The cover screen and the inner screen keep separate layouts. Widgets sized for the narrow screen stay there, and the wide arrangement is already set up when you open the phone.

App Blocks

Smart-home scenes
under the Home icon.

Name your Google Home routines once and they sit under the Home icon. Turning the lights off on the way out is a press and a tap, instead of opening the app and waiting for it to load.

  • Rename each scene to whatever you call it
  • Reorder them so the one you use most is first
  • Pull any scene out as its own home screen tile

Widgets

Stacked, resized, or inside an icon.

Several widgets can share one slot; swipe to bring the next forward. Any widget can be resized or zoomed without leaving the home screen.

A widget inside the icon

Give an app a widget and it runs in that app's panel instead of taking a slot on the home screen. One widget per app, compact or large.

Control

App Zero

Game launcher

Pick a launcher app, then choose which games appear in its panel. The library sits under one icon.

Hide what you never open

Hidden apps leave home, the dock and search. They stay installed and still work.

Notification history

Kept per app, with the images they arrived with, after the shade has been cleared. Clear one app or all of them.

Backup and restore

Pages, folders, widgets and settings saved to a file you keep. New phone, same home screen.

◱

Folds properly

Cover and inner screens keep their own layouts, so opening the phone does not rearrange anything.

Privacy

Arcadia has no network permission.
There is no route off the phone.

Arcadia never asks for network access, so Android refuses any outbound connection it could attempt. Your notifications, your layout and your location stay on the phone.

No network permission No analytics No ads No account No precise location No file access

Everything Arcadia stores goes when you uninstall it. Read the policy.
