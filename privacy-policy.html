Arcadia — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 29 September 2026

Arcadia is a home screen launcher for Android, published by Arcadia Foundry.

The short version

Arcadia does not collect your data. Arcadia has no internet permission, so it is

technically incapable of sending anything anywhere. There are no analytics, no

advertising, no crash reporting services, no accounts and no third-party SDKs

that transmit data.

Everything Arcadia reads stays on your device.

What Arcadia reads, and why

Your installed apps. Arcadia is a launcher, so it reads the list of apps

installed on your device in order to show them, let you search them, arrange them

on your home screen and put them in folders. This list never leaves your device.

Your notifications — only if you turn it on. If you grant Arcadia

notification access, it reads notifications so it can show badges on app icons

and a notification list on the home screen. Notification content, including

recent notification history, is stored on your device only, in Arcadia's own

private app storage. It is never transmitted. Revoking notification access in

Android settings stops this immediately, and uninstalling Arcadia deletes it.

Accessibility service — only if you turn it on, and only to lock the screen.

Arcadia offers an optional double-tap-to-lock gesture. Android only allows an app

to lock the screen this way through the accessibility service, so if you want

that gesture you have to enable it. Arcadia uses it for exactly one thing: to

perform the lock action. It does not read screen content, it does not read text

you type, it does not monitor other apps, and it does not record anything. This

is not an accessibility tool and is not presented as one. If you do not want the

gesture, do not enable the service; every other Arcadia feature works without it.

Your home screen layout and settings. Icon positions, folders, widget

placement, and your preferences are saved in Arcadia's private app storage so

your home screen survives a restart.

What Arcadia does not do

  • No data collection, and no data sharing with anyone
  • No internet access — the INTERNET permission is not declared
  • No analytics, telemetry, advertising or crash-reporting services
  • No accounts, no sign-in, no cloud sync
  • No precise location — ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION is not declared
  • No selling or transferring of data, because none is gathered

Approximate location

Arcadia declares ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION for one feature: the sunrise and sunset

times shown when you long-press a clock app. Sunrise and sunset shift by seconds

across a city, so city-level accuracy is all it asks for and all it uses.

The coordinates are used on the device to compute those two times and are cached in

Arcadia's private app storage so the card does not have to re-locate you constantly.

They are never sent anywhere — Arcadia does not declare the INTERNET permission

and contains no network code, so transmitting them is not technically possible.

Decline the permission and the feature falls back to a default location; nothing

else changes. You can revoke it at any time in Android Settings → Apps → Arcadia →

Permissions.

Widgets from other apps

Arcadia can host widgets provided by other apps you have installed. Those widgets

are rendered by the apps that own them and follow their own privacy policies, not

this one. Arcadia does not read their contents.

Children

Arcadia is a general-purpose launcher and is not directed at children. It gathers

no personal information from anyone, including children.

Your control

  • Notification access, accessibility service: revoke at any time in Android

Settings, and the associated feature simply stops.

  • All Arcadia data: removed when you uninstall the app, or via Android Settings →

Apps → Arcadia → Storage → Clear storage.

Changes

If this policy changes, the "Last updated" date above changes with it, and the

current version is always at this URL.
